# Quassar Event Engine

**Event-Driven Quantitative Research | Financial Event Analysis | Market Reactions | Temporal Data Modeling**

## Overview

Quassar Event Engine is a quantitative research and computational engineering initiative within Quassar Capital, focused on the mathematical representation, statistical analysis, and interpretation of financial and macroeconomic events and their relationships with market behavior.

Its research scope encompasses economic announcements, monetary policy decisions, corporate disclosures, earnings releases, credit events, regulatory developments, and other information-driven changes in financial markets.

The methodological framework emphasizes temporal accuracy, statistical identification, economic interpretation, numerical reliability, reproducibility, and disciplined assessment of uncertainty and model limitations.

## Research Domains

### Financial Event Identification & Classification

Quantitative methods for defining, categorizing, and analyzing financial and macroeconomic events.

Research topics include event taxonomies, announcement characteristics, information timestamps, event relevance, event clustering, and the distinction between scheduled announcements and unexpected developments.

Event classification emphasizes consistent definitions, verifiable source information, and the separation of observed facts from analytical interpretations.

### Event Studies & Abnormal Return Analysis

Statistical approaches to evaluating financial market behavior around identifiable events.

Areas of interest include event windows, estimation windows, expected return models, abnormal returns, cumulative abnormal returns, statistical significance, and the interpretation of market reactions.

Research methodology accounts for overlapping events, event-induced volatility, model specification, cross-sectional dependence, and the limitations of causal interpretation.

### Macroeconomic Announcements & Market Responses

Quantitative methods for examining financial market reactions to economic information and monetary policy developments.

Research topics include inflation releases, employment data, economic growth indicators, central bank decisions, policy communications, and changes in market expectations.

Analytical interpretation distinguishes announced information from unexpected components relative to prevailing expectations, recognizing that market responses depend on the information already reflected in asset prices.

### Corporate Events & Financial Disclosures

Statistical methods for analyzing financial market behavior surrounding corporate announcements and disclosures.

Areas of interest include earnings releases, corporate guidance, capital structure changes, mergers and acquisitions announcements, dividend decisions, and other material corporate developments.

Research emphasizes announcement timing, information availability, event overlap, market expectations, and the distinction between disclosure effects and broader market movements.

### Temporal Data Modeling & Event Sequences

Mathematical and computational approaches to representing time-dependent financial information and sequences of market events.

Research encompasses irregularly spaced observations, temporal alignment, event-time analysis, point processes, sequential dependencies, and the statistical properties of event-driven datasets.

Particular attention is given to timestamp precision, publication delays, information revisions, and the prevention of look-ahead bias.

### Market Reaction & Information Transmission

Quantitative methods for examining the relationship between new information, asset prices, volatility, liquidity, and trading activity.

Research topics include price adjustment, volatility responses, cross-asset transmission, information diffusion, market microstructure effects, and the persistence or reversal of observed market reactions.

Analytical interpretation distinguishes statistical associations from causal effects and accounts for concurrent information, endogenous market behavior, and changes in prevailing financial conditions.

### Event-Based Statistical Modeling

Mathematical and statistical approaches to analyzing conditional financial outcomes associated with defined event characteristics.

Areas of interest include conditional distributions, regression methods, probabilistic classification, time-series models, uncertainty quantification, and the evaluation of event-related statistical relationships.

Research emphasizes model identification, parameter stability, appropriate benchmark selection, and the limitations of historical relationships as representations of future outcomes.

### Event Data Integrity & Source Verification

Computational and methodological approaches to maintaining accurate event definitions, source attribution, and temporal consistency.

Research topics include authoritative source selection, timestamp normalization, duplicate identification, event revisions, data provenance, information availability, and reproducible event records.

Particular attention is given to distinguishing the time of an underlying event from the time of publication, redistribution, ingestion, or subsequent reporting.

## Quantitative Research Methodology

The quantitative research methodology is organized around the following principles:

- **Event Definition:** Clearly specified event categories, observation units, announcement characteristics, and analytical inclusion criteria.
- **Temporal Integrity:** Accurate treatment of event timestamps, information availability, publication delays, revisions, and event sequencing.
- **Economic Interpretation:** Explicit consideration of market expectations, information surprises, concurrent developments, and the economic significance of observed reactions.
- **Mathematical Rigor:** Consistent model formulations, well-defined variables, defensible assumptions, and appropriate analytical methods.
- **Statistical Identification:** Careful consideration of confounding events, selection bias, endogeneity, overlapping observations, and the distinction between association and causation.
- **Statistical Reliability:** Appropriate estimation procedures, diagnostic testing, uncertainty quantification, and recognition of finite-sample limitations.
- **Numerical Verification:** Evaluation of computational accuracy, algorithmic stability, and numerical approximation error.
- **Out-of-Sample Evaluation:** Disciplined separation of estimation and evaluation samples, with safeguards against look-ahead bias, information leakage, and retrospective event selection.
- **Reproducibility:** Documented methodologies, controlled dependencies, traceable event records, and repeatable analytical experiments.
- **Model Validation:** Benchmark comparisons, statistical diagnostics, sensitivity analysis, and explicit assessment of methodological limitations.
- **Responsible Research:** Protection of confidential financial information, proprietary methodologies, restricted datasets, and sensitive analytical materials.

## Technology & Computational Infrastructure

The computational scope encompasses programming languages and numerical technologies relevant to event data processing, temporal analysis, statistical modeling, and scientific computing.

Technology selection is determined by the analytical requirements of each application rather than by a prescribed implementation stack.

Relevant technologies include:

- **Python:** Financial event analysis, statistical modeling, temporal data processing, numerical research, and analytical visualization.
- **C++:** Performance-sensitive numerical algorithms, computational kernels, and large-scale event data processing.
- **Rust:** Systems programming, memory-safe computational components, and reliable data-processing architecture.
- **Julia:** Statistical computing, mathematical modeling, numerical optimization, and scientific analysis.
- **GPU-Accelerated Computing:** Parallel numerical workloads, matrix-intensive computation, and computationally demanding statistical procedures where technically justified.

Core engineering principles include modular architecture, version control, automated testing, numerical regression analysis, benchmark evaluation, scientific documentation, and reproducible computational environments.

## Research Governance & Model Integrity

The research governance framework is defined by methodological transparency, temporal accuracy, statistical discipline, information security, and responsible handling of financial research materials.

### Research Integrity

Event definitions, source information, statistical relationships, computational procedures, and analytical conclusions require accurate representation, with appropriate consideration of uncertainty and methodological limitations.

### Model Verification & Validation

Event-based models are evaluated according to suitable numerical verification methods, statistical diagnostics, benchmark comparisons, sensitivity analysis, and validation criteria relevant to their intended analytical applications.

### Temporal & Information Integrity

Research interpretation accounts for the distinction between event occurrence, initial public disclosure, subsequent reporting, data ingestion, and later revisions.

Analytical procedures must not treat information as available before its actual release or otherwise introduce information from the future into historical evaluations.

### Statistical Robustness

Research interpretation accounts for event clustering, overlapping observation windows, model specification risk, multiple testing, selection effects, changing market conditions, and the instability of historical statistical relationships.

### Data Governance

Research data is subject to appropriate quality controls, source attribution, provenance requirements, licensing restrictions, confidentiality considerations, and access limitations.

### Information Security

Proprietary investment signals, confidential event classifications, internal research parameters, restricted datasets, and production financial infrastructure are excluded from public research materials.

### Intellectual Property

Public research materials are distinguished from proprietary methodologies, confidential information, licensed datasets, and intellectual property belonging to Quassar Capital or third parties.

## Scope of Public Materials

Public disclosure of research materials is subject to technical, institutional, intellectual property, data licensing, and information security review.

Materials eligible for public distribution may include general-purpose event study methodologies, nonproprietary statistical models, reproducible numerical examples, synthetic event datasets, educational financial simulations, and technical documentation.

Confidential investment signals, proprietary event classification methodologies, restricted datasets, internal research parameters, trading strategies, and proprietary financial infrastructure are excluded from public disclosure.
